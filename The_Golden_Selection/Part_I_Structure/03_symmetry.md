## Pillar 1: Dimensional Maximization [PROVEN]

### The 3D vs "2D+1D" Distinction

| QC Type | Structure | Information Density |
|---------|-----------|---------------------|
| **Icosahedral (H₃)** | Aperiodic in ALL 3 directions | Isotropic, volumetric |
| Decagonal (D₁₀h) | 2D aperiodic + 1D periodic | "Cylindrical" — dilute in z |
| Dodecagonal (D₁₂h) | 2D aperiodic + 1D periodic | Same deficit |
| Octagonal (D₈h) | 2D aperiodic + 1D periodic | Same deficit |

### The Argument

Axial quasicrystals (decagonal, octagonal, dodecagonal) are essentially **stacked 2D quasicrystals**. They have:
- Aperiodicity in the xy-plane
- **Periodicity along the z-axis** (typically 4-16 Å repeat)

This creates an **information density deficit**: the z-direction contributes no non-redundant structure.

**Icosahedral QCs** are fundamentally different:
- Aperiodic in ALL THREE directions
- No direction of periodicity
- Generated by 6D → 3D projection (not 5D → 2D + 1D)
- Every atom's position determined by full 3D intersection

> **Conclusion**: If the axiom maximizes generative information density per unit volume, it must select the structure that avoids periodicity in ALL directions. Only H₃ qualifies.

## Pillar 2: Thermodynamic Selection [PROVEN]

### Energetic vs. Entropic Stability

| Stability Type | Mechanism | At T → 0 | Examples |
|----------------|-----------|----------|----------|
| **Energetic** | Enthalpy minimization | ✅ Stable | i-Al-Cu-Fe, i-Al-Pd-Mn |
| **Entropic** | Random tiling ensemble | ❌ Decomposes | d-Al-Ni-Co, d-Al-Cu-Co |

### The Critical Distinction

**Icosahedral QCs (many)**: Stabilized by **enthalpy** (energy minimization)
- Hume-Rothery mechanism: Fermi surface / pseudo-Brillouin zone resonance
- Creates pseudogap at Fermi level → lowers total electronic energy
- Remains stable as T → 0
- High structural perfection (low phason strain)

**Axial QCs (many)**: Stabilized by **entropy** (random tiling)
- Many degenerate tile configurations
- Phason flips are nearly free energetically
- At high T: entropy term (TS) stabilizes disorder
- At T → 0: **decomposes into crystalline approximants**

### Natural Evidence: Khatyrka Meteorite

|---------|----------|-----------|--------|
| **Icosahedrite** | Icosahedral | Equilibrium | Primary, stable billions of years |

> **Conclusion**: If "stable" in the axiom means true ground state (stable at T=0), then entropic random tilings are eliminated. Only H₃ phases qualify as energetically stable quasicrystals.

## Pillar 3: Golden Lock-in Saturation [PROVEN]

### Bruna (2025): The Mechanism

Bruna's paper "Schur-Convex Curvature on Dihedral Exponential Families and the Golden-Ratio Stationary Point" proves:

1. Systems with D₁₂ symmetry have Schur-convex curvature
2. This curvature has a **unique stationary point** (minimum)
3. The minimum is exactly at **φ⁻² = (3-√5)/2**
4. This is the "Golden Lock-in" — the golden ratio as geometric necessity

### From D₁₂ to H₃: Dimensional Extension

| Symmetry | Golden Lock-in | Coverage |
|----------|----------------|----------|
| **D₁₂** (dodecagonal) | φ⁻² is curvature minimum | **2D plane only** |
| **H₃** (icosahedral) | Same mechanism | **All 3D space** |

**Key insight**: The icosahedral group H₃ is the **maximal spatial realization** of the golden lock-in:

- In D₁₂: The golden ratio stability operates in ONE plane; the z-direction is unconstrained
- In H₃: Multiple intersecting 5-fold axes enforce the constraint in ALL directions simultaneously

> **Conclusion**: H₃ saturates Bruna's stability mechanism across the full 3D manifold. A D₁₂ system is "half-stable" (2D); an H₃ system is "fully stable" (3D).

## Pillar 4: Topological Protection [ESTABLISHED]

### Phason Space Topology

| QC Type | Phason Space Dimension | Topology | Protection |
|---------|------------------------|----------|------------|
| Axial (D₁₀h, D₁₂h) | 2D | **Torus T²** | Reducible, "flat" |
| **Icosahedral (H₃)** | 3D | **3-sphere S³** | Hopf fibrations |

### The Hopf Advantage

Icosahedral QCs have phason space with S³ topology, which admits:
- **Hopf fibrations** (S³ → S²): linked circles filling the 3-sphere
- Non-trivial π₃(S³) = ℤ allows knotted/twisted textures
- Robust topological protection against perturbations

Axial QCs have toroidal (T² = S¹ × S¹) phason space:
- Reducible to product of circles
- "Flat" topology with weaker protection
- Defects classified by simpler homotopy

> **Conclusion**: The structural information in H₃ QCs is protected by richer topology — not just "written" in atomic positions but "secured" by Hopf linking.

## Background: Crystallographic Restriction

### The Theorem [KNOWN]

> **Crystallographic Restriction Theorem**:
> 
> A periodic lattice in D dimensions can only have rotational symmetries of order 1, 2, 3, 4, or 6.

**5-fold symmetry is forbidden in crystals.**

### Consequence

Quasicrystals, being aperiodic, are **not bound** by this restriction. They can have 5-fold, 8-fold, 10-fold, 12-fold, or icosahedral symmetry.

## The Golden Ratio in H₃

### Algebraic Origin [KNOWN]

The H₃ group is intimately connected to the golden ratio:

$$\phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 1.618$$

This connection arises because:

1. **Pentagon**: The ratio diagonal/side = φ
2. **Icosahedron**: Edge lengths involve φ (vertices at (0, ±1, ±φ) and permutations)
3. **Rotation eigenvalues**: 5-fold rotation has eigenvalues involving $\cos(72°) = \frac{\phi^{-1}}{2}$

### Not Assumed — Derived

1. Maximize ρ_G → aperiodic order (I.A)
