## 2. DEFINITIONS

### 2.1 Statistical Complexity ($C_\mu$)

**Reference**: Crutchfield & Young (1989), Shalizi (2001)

Statistical Complexity is the Shannon entropy of a system's **causal states** (the minimal ε-machine):

$$C_\mu = H[\mathcal{S}] = -\sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} P(s) \log P(s)$$

Where $\mathcal{S}$ is the set of causal states — the minimal partition of past histories that yield identical conditional probabilities for future observables.

- **Periodic sequence**: Finite causal states → $C_\mu$ bounded
- **Random sequence**: No predictive structure → $C_\mu = 0$
- **Fibonacci sequence**: Infinite causal states (claimed) → $C_\mu \to \infty$

### 2.2 Inflation Symmetry (Substitution Rules)

A tiling/sequence has **inflation symmetry** if there exists a substitution rule $\sigma$ such that:
- $\sigma$ maps each tile/letter to a finite pattern of tiles/letters
- The tiling is a fixed point of iterated application of $\sigma$
- The inflation factor $\lambda > 1$ scales lengths

**Example**: Fibonacci word
- Rule: $A \to AB$, $B \to A$
- Inflation factor: $\lambda = \phi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$

### 2.3 Other Aperiodic Structures

**Incommensurate crystals**: A periodic base structure with an incommensurate modulation wave. Example: charge density waves with irrational wavevector.

**Limit-periodic structures**: Obtained as limits of periodic approximants. Example: the "chair" tiling limit.

**Sturmian sequences**: Sequences with complexity function $p(n) = n+1$. Some are generated by substitutions (e.g., Fibonacci), but others are not (e.g., Sturmian sequences with non-quadratic irrational slopes).
