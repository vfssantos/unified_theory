# 11. The Higgs Mass

## Summary

This section derives the Higgs mass from dodecahedral geometry:

### 11.1 The Geometric Setup

- **Fermion vertex**: v_F = (1, 1, 1), |v_F|² = 3
- **Higgs vertex**: v_H = (0, φ, 1/φ), |v_H|² = φ² + φ⁻² = 3
- **Both on same sphere**: Radius √3 in the dodecahedron

### 11.2 The Geometric Coupling

- **Dot product**: 
  $$\mathbf{v}_F \cdot \mathbf{v}_H = 0 + \varphi + \varphi^{-1} = \sqrt{5}$$
- **Cosine of angle**:
  $$\cos\theta = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$$

### 11.3 Interpretation as Yukawa Factor

- **Claim**: This geometric angle determines the Higgs-top coupling
- **Relation**: 
  $$\lambda_{\text{geom}} = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{3} \approx 0.745$$

### 11.4 Mass Prediction

- **Tree-level formula**:
  $$m_H^{\text{tree}} = \lambda_{\text{geom}} \cdot m_t = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{3} \times 172.8 \text{ GeV} \approx 128.7 \text{ GeV}$$
- **Observed**: m_H ≈ 125.1 GeV
- **Discrepancy**: ~3%, attributed to RG running and loop corrections

### 11.5 Critical Assessment

**Problems with this derivation**:
1. Why should a geometric angle equal a mass ratio?
2. In SM, m_H depends on λ (self-coupling), not Yukawa
3. The relation m_H ~ y_t × v is not standard
4. This is interpretation, not derivation

**Possible resolution**:
- In this framework, all couplings are geometric
- The Higgs potential may be constrained by the same geometry
- Need to show V(Φ) emerges from dodecahedral structure

### 11.6 Alternative View

- Perhaps √5/3 is a coincidence
- Or it's a hint of deeper structure not yet understood
- Falsifiable: Precision measurements of m_H, m_t, and their ratio
